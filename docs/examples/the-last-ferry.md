# The city before dawn

At six in the morning, the city belonged to the people who had somewhere else to be.

Derya belonged to neither shore. She carried her small collection of tickets like proof that a person could leave and return without becoming quite the same.

The letter was waiting on her usual seat. It had no stamp, no address, only her name in a hand she recognised before she remembered why.

## 06:10 · Karaköy

The gulls arrived before the light.

Derya stood at the end of the pier with her notebook pressed against her coat. The water was the colour of an old spoon. Across the strait, a few windows held the last of the night.

She had made this crossing a thousand times, but she still counted the ropes as they fell away. One. Two. Three. A small ceremony for leaving.

Behind her, the ticket keeper was folding a newspaper into a shape it had never intended to become. He nodded without looking up.

“Same seat?”

“For now,” she said.

The engine answered before either of them could think of anything else. Somewhere beneath the deck, the city let go.

# The shore we return to

By the time she reached the other side, the letter had changed the shape of the morning.
